Overriding the System: Navigating the Digital Canvas

Curated by Karan Khatri

About the Exhibition

Usually, when we go online we don't think about the web browser or the computer screen. We just care about the videos or text we are looking at. The artists in this show do the exact opposite. They want you to look at the parts of the computer we normally ignore, turning the computer itself into the artwork.

For example, Jodi.org takes away all normal web design so you just see the messy code that builds the site. Olia Lialina uses the separate boxes of old web browsers to break up a story, so you have to click through them to read it. Windows 93 is a fake computer desktop that you can click around and play with.

Instead of just being a tool to look at other things, the website becomes the main attraction. By making visitors figure out how to use these unusual sites, the artists show that making a website can be just as creative as painting a picture or any other artwork online.

Windows 93

Artist: Jankenpopp & Zombectro | Year: 2014

This website looks and acts like an old computer from the 1990s. You can click around the desktop to open fake programs, find hidden files, and play simple games right in your browser.

My Boyfriend Came Back From The War

Artist: Olia Lialina | Year: 1996

This artwork tells a story about a couple talking after a war. It uses the separate boxes of early web browsers to break the story into different pieces that you have to click through.

Jodi.org

Artist: JODI (Joan Heemskerk and Dirk Paesmans) | Year: 1995

This website takes away all normal design and just shows you the raw HTML code that builds the page. It makes you look at the hidden parts of a website instead of a clean UI.
